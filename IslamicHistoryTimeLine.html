A Timeline: Prophet & Rashedeen
Important Dates of the Islamic expansion
rapid military & cultural advances, power
struggle, dynistic rules and civil wars
From Prophet Muhammad to Rashedeen
Photo courtesy of Llara-Jameson via Pexels License
2000 BC
Prophet Ibrahim, was put on fire
by King Namrud in the city of Ur
Mesapotamia, when he defied
his claim of being GOD
Photo courtesy of Ricardo Gomez Angel on Unsplash
520 CE
Ethiopian King Abraha Attacked Mecca.
His forces & Elephents were crushed
to Death from stones, dropped by birds
Which all of sudden appeared on the sky
Photo courtesy of Abu Al-balami via Public Domain
570 CE
Prophet Muhammad was born
as an orphan in the city of Mecca
in the tribe Banu-Hashim, who were
the custodians of Kaaba at that time
Photo courtesy of Wikimedia via Public domain Wikimedia Commons
610 CE
First revelation received from
Angel Gabriel on Mount Hira
Where Prophet used to come regularly
to think about GOD
Photo courtesy of Musaddek Sayek via Pexels License
613 CE
Prophet Muhammad begins public
preaching in Mecca and was severely
opposed by city elites, except few poor
who dared to accept Islam risking lives.
Photo courtesy of zeynep-sude-emek via Pexels License
622 CE
Prophet Muhammad receives invitation
from Madina and avails this opportunity
to establish the first Islamic state in the
world. Islamic Calendar starts from here.
Photo courtesy of Marie Lemaistre via Pexels License
624-627 CE
Key battles including Badr, Uhad and
the Battle of the Trench are fought
between Muslims and non-beleivers.
victories in battles consolidated Islam.
Photo courtesy of Adiput Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons
630 CE
Mecca was conquered; Islam gets fully
established in the Arabian Peninsula
Idols were removed from Kaaba. Haj was
performed first time in the Islamic way.
Photo courtesy of Muhammad Sadiq Bey via Wikimedia Commons CC BY-SA 3.0
632 CE
Prophet Muhammad passes away,
Abu Bakr becomes the first Caliph
initiating the Rashidun Caliphate
which lasts for the next 30 years
Photo courtesy of Konard via Pexels License
632-661 CE
Khilafate-Rashdeen for the next 30 years
with expansion into the neighbouring
powerful Byzantine & Sassanid territories
and the first civil war among Muslims.
Photo courtesy of Wario2 CC0, via Wikimedia Commons
632 CE
Prophet's ally Abu Bakr As-siddiq takes over
after being elected as the first Khalifa,
immediately dispatched prophet's
incompleted expedition to Syrian border.
Photo courtesy of Mark Ahsmann, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons
633 CE
Rebellions break out across Arabia
as several tribes refused to pay Zakat or
followed false prophets. force dispatched
to control the rebellions to follow rules
Courtesy of Mohammad adil at the English-language Wikipedia, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons
633 CE
First Compilation of the Quran
Following high casualties of memorizers Zyad
ibn Thabit was ordered to collect & compile
Quran into a single and authentic volume.
Courtesy of AnonymousUnknown author, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons
633-634 CE
War with neighbouring super powers
Prophet's earlier coordinated military
campaigns were launched into the
borders of Persian & Byzantine Empires.
Courtesy of Heretiq, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons
634 CE
First Khalifa Passes away after
nominating Umar ibn al-Khattab to
succeed him as the next leader of
the newly formed Islamic state.
Courtesy of i_pinz from Vienna, AT, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons
634 CE
Prophet's ally Umar bin Khattab takes over
His caliphate period lasts for 10 years
with unprecedent victories over great
empires of Byzantine and Sassanid
Photo courtesy of Bassem, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons
634 CE
Battle of Ajnadayn.
A major decisive clash resulting in a Rashidun
victory over the Byzantine forces in Palestine
opening the door of Conquests in Levant
Courtesy of Yoav Dothan, GFDL, via Wikimedia Commons
636 CE
Battle of Yarmouk.
A monumental six-day battle where Muslim
forces defeat the Byzantine army under
tactical command of Khālid ibn al-Walīd
Courtesy of Mohammad adil at the English-language Wikipedia, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons
636 CE
Battle of al-Qadisiyyah
Led by Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, the Muslim
forces decisively defeat Sasanian Persians,
cracking Persian control over Iraq.
Courtesy of Mohammad adil at the English-language Wikipedia, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons
639-642 CE
Conquest of Egypt.
Amr ibn al-Aas leads the Egypt campaign
captures historical Babylon and Alexandria,
ending Byzantine & starting Muslim rule.
By Mohammad Adilderivative work: Freedom's Falcon, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons
642 CE
Battle of Nahavand.
"Victory of Victories," won by Muslim army
under Al-Numan ibn Muqarrin. This war gave
Sasanian empire a catastrophic defeat
By Eugène Flandin, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons
644 CE
Assassination of Umar ibn Al-Khattab.
Was mortally wounded while leading prayer.
Before passing away he appointed a six-man
committee (Shura) to choose the next Caliph
By Mohammad adil at English Wikipedia, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons
644 CE
Uthman ibn Affan is chosen as third Caliph.
His caliphate lasted 12 years from 644 to 656
First phase of major expansion and prosperity
followed by phase of rising internal dissent
By Vikiçizer, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons
647-649 CE
Formation of the First Muslim Navy.
Authorizes Muawiyah(governor of Syria)
to build the first Islamic naval fleet,
leading to the conquest of Cyprus.
Courtesy of Cattette, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons
651 CE
standardized the Qur'an between 650-656.
created an authoritative written text and
distributed it across the Islamic empire
to prevent regional disputes over recitation.
Courtesy of Wiggum, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons
651-654 CE
Islamic state expansions continues east-west
Military expeditions push deeper into
North Africa, coastal parts of Europe,
and across Central Asia.
Courtesy of Jean-Pierre Dalbéra from Paris, France, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons
656 CE
Assassination of Uthman ibn Affan
Political factions, regional discontents result
in besiege of Uthman's house in Medina and
his assassination while reading the Quran.
Courtesy of Topkapı Palace Museum Library, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons
656 CE
Ascension to the Caliphate
Ali ibn Abi Talib elected as the fourth Caliph
amidst heavy political turmoil in Yathrub.
He shifts capital from Yathrub to Kufa, Iraq.
Courtesy of Bjelica, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons
656 CE
Battle of the Camel.
Demand of justice for Uthman's assassination
The first civil conflict occurs near Basra
Fourth Khalifa emerges victorious and restores order.
Courtesy of محک, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons
657 CE
Battle of the Siffin.
Battle between fourth Khalifa and Muawiyah
(governor of Syria) ended in acceptance
from both sides of a political arbitration.
Courtesy of The original uploader was Zeledi at English Wikipedia., CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons
658 CE
Battle of Nahrawan.
A radical faction called Kharijites breaks away
from Khalifa after rejecting the arbitration
announced during the battle of siffin
Courtesy of Gertrude Bell (verstorben 1926), Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons
661 CE
End of Rashedeen Chaliphate.
Assassiniated outside a mosque in Kufa.
Killed by a Kharijite al-Rahman ibn Muljam.
Hasan ibn Ali, succeeds but abdicates soon,
to avoid further bloodshed and division.
Courtesy of See page for author, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons
