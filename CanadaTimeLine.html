Canada TimeLine
From Indigineous civilization, to European contacts, to a modern world state

Photograph By Boaventuravinicius, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

30000–15000 BC The First Migration Through Beringa
First peoples cross into North America from Asia, establishing rich and diverse Indigenous societies.

Photograph By Suevic, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

5000 BC - 4000 BC Paleo-Inuit
A later migration brought the ancestors of the Paleo-Inuit from Siberia settling northern coasts of Canada & Greenland.

Photograph By Evgenii Salganik, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

1000 CE First Migration From Europe
Norse Explorers, reached Canada and colonized parts of Newfoundland making the first known European contact.

Photograph By Hans Dahl, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

>
1497 CE John Cabot
was an Italian explorer, sailed under the commission of King Henry VII of England making landfall in Newfoundland.

Photograph By Evan T Jones, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

1534 CE Jacques Cartier
Explores the Gulf of St. Lawrence and claims the land for France using the local word kanata.

Photograph By Jon Platek, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

1608 CE Samuel de Champlain
founds Québec City as a permanent French settlement and the capital of New France.

Photograph By Ambroise Louis Garneray, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

>
1670 CE Hudsons Bay Company
The British Crown grants a charter to the Hudson's Bay Company, expanding the fur trade.

Photograph By Lkovac, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

>
1759 CE Plains of Abraham
British forces defeat the French at the Battle of the Plains of Abraham in Qubec.

Photograph By Gilbert Bochenek, CC BY 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

>
1763 CE The Treaty of Paris
formally cedes New France to British control, turning into a British colony and ends French authority on Qubec.

Photograph By Jon Platek, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

>
1791 CE The Constitutional Act
Divides Quebec into two divisions Upper Canada English-speaking, Lower Canada French-speaking.

Photograph By Piotron, CC BY 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

>
1812-1814 CE U.S Invasions
Canadian and British forces successfully repel the U.S. Invasion.

Photograph By RiverBrink Art Museum, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

1867 CE Canada Formed
Unites Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia and New Brunswick into the federal Dominion of Canada.

Photograph By Gilbert Bochenek, CC BY 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

>
1870-1873 CE Provinces Join the federation
Manitoba, British Columbia, and Prince Edward Island join the new confederation.

Photograph By Charles Hoffman, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

>
1885 CE The Pacific Railway
The transcontinental Railway is completed linking the country from coast to coast.

Photograph By Photograph by David R. Spencer., CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

>
1905 CE More Join the federation
Alberta and Saskatchewan join the federal Dominion of Canada as Provinces.

Photograph By Golbez, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

>
1914-1918 CE World War I
Canada fights alongside the Allies establishing a strong international identity.

Photograph By RiverBrink Art Museum, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

1931 CE Legislative independence
Statute of Westminster grants Canada legislative independence from the British Parliament.

Photograph By Benson Kua, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

1939-1945 CE World War II
Canada plays a major role in World War II and experiencese a massive postwar economic boom.

Photograph By Nenad Martinovič, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

>
1982 CE Constitution Act
Queen Elizabeth II signs the Act Patriating Canada constitution and establish the Charter of Freedom.

Photograph By Harvey K from Toronto, Canada, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

>
1999 CE Third territory
Nunavut is established as third territory, separating from the Northwest Territories.

Photograph By The Cosmonaut, CC BY-SA 2.5 CA, via Wikimedia Commons
