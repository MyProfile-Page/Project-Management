01. Canada TimeLine

From Indigineous civilization. To European contacts To a modern world state.

Photograph By Jason Pratt, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons
30,000–15,000 BC
The First Migration Through Beringa
First peoples cross into North America
from Asia, establishing rich
and diverse Indigenous societies.
Photograph By NASA/GSFC/JPL/MISR-Team, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons
5,000 BC - 4,000 BC
Paleo-Inuit
A later migration brought the ancestors
of the Paleo-Inuit from Siberia settling
northern coasts of Canada & Greenland.
Photograph By Evgenii Salganik, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons
1000 CE
First Migration From Europe
Norse Explorers, reached Canada and
colonized parts of Newfoundland making
the first known European contact.
Photograph By Hans Dahl Hans Dahl, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons
1,497 CE
John Cabot
was an Italian explorer, sailed under the
commission of King Henry VII of England
making landfall in Newfoundland.
Photograph By Evan T Jones, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons
1,534 CE
Jacques Cartier
Explores the Gulf of St. Lawrence
and claims the land for France
using the local word kanata.
Photograph By Jon Platek, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons
1,608 CE
Samuel de Champlain
founds Québec City as a permanent
French settlement and the capital
of New France.
Photograph By Ambroise Louis Garneray, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons
1,670 CE
Hudsons Bay Company
The British Crown grants a charter
to the Hudson's Bay Company,
expanding the fur trade.
Photograph By Lkovac, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons
1,759 CE
Plains of Abraham
British forces defeat the French
at the Battle of the Plains of Abraham ,
in Qubec.
Photograph ByThomas Jefferys, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons
1,763 CE
The Treaty of Paris
formally cedes New France to British
control, turning into a British colony
and ends French authority on Qubec.
Photograph By Les Frères Maristes, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons
1,791 CE
The Constitutional Act
divides Quebec into two divisions
Upper Canada English-speaking,
Lower Canada French-speaking.
Photograph By Piotron, CC BY 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons
1812-1814 CE
U.S Invasions
Canadian and British forces
successfully repel
the U.S. invasion.
Photograph By RiverBrink Art Museum, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons
1867 CE
Canada Formed
Unites Ontario, Quebec,
Nova Scotia and New Brunswick
into the federal Dominion of Canada.
Photograph By Golbez Golbez, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons
1870-1873 CE
Provinces Join the federation
Manitoba, British Columbia,
and Prince Edward Island
join the new confederation.
Photograph By Golbez assumed, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons
1885 CE
The Pacific Railway
The transcontinental Railway
is completed linking
the country from coast to coast.
Photograph by David R. Spencer., CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons
1905 CE
More Join the federation
Alberta and Saskatchewan
join the federal Dominion
of Canada as Provinces.
Photograph By Golbez, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons
1914-1918 CE
World War I
Canada fights alongside the
Allies establishing a strong
international identity.
Photograph By Willy Stöwer, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons
1931 CE
Legislative independence
Statute of Westminster grants
Canada legislative independence
from the British Parliament.
Photograph By Benson Kua, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons
1939-1945 CE
World War II
Canada plays a major role
in World War II and experiencese
a massive postwar economic boom.
By Brock F J (Fg Off), Royal Air Force official photographer, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons
1982 CE
Constitution Act
Queen Elizabeth II signs the Act
Patriating Canada constitution and
establish the Charter of Freedom.
Photograph By Harvey K from Toronto, Canada, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons
1999 CE
Third territory
Nunavut is established
as third territory, separating
from the Northwest Territories.
Photograph By The Cosmonaut, CC BY-SA 2.5 CA, via Wikimedia Commons
